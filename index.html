Under the hood

How the magic works

Photos — content-aware inpainting

  1. 1Mask it. Paint or box the watermark. The red overlay marks pixels to rebuild.
  2. 2Reconstruct. Colors flood inward from the mask boundary, then dozens of diffusion passes blend them into a seamless patch.
  3. 3Refine & save. Repeat on stubborn spots, undo anytime, download as PNG.

Videos — real-time region processing

  1. 1Box the logo. Drag a rectangle over the watermark — it stays fixed for the whole clip.
  2. 2Pick an effect. Blur, pixelate or smooth-fill every frame at full playback speed, audio intact.
  3. 3Export. The cleaned canvas is re-recorded with your original audio into a downloadable WebM.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Everything — masking, inpainting, video processing and export — runs entirely in JavaScript inside your browser. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads.

What results should I expect?

Inpainting works best on smooth or textured backgrounds (sky, grass, walls, skin). Complex structures behind a large watermark may look softened — use several small passes instead of one giant mask. For video, blur and smooth-fill make logos effectively invisible on most footage.

How do credits work?

Sign in with Google and you get 6 free credits every day. Each photo removal costs 1 credit and each video export costs 6 credits. Credits refill to 6 automatically at midnight — masking, previewing and comparing are always free.

Is removing watermarks legal?

Only remove watermarks from media you own or have permission to edit — for example your own exports from trial software, or licensed stock you've purchased. Removing copyright marks from others' work to republish it may violate copyright law.

Why does video export take as long as the clip?

The browser re-records the processed frames in real time with the original audio track, so a 30-second clip takes about 30 seconds. Keep the tab visible while it records for smooth output.
